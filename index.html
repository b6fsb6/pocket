      How it works
      
      
      The app plays a click and listens to your pad through the microphone. It matches each hit to the stroke that was due and measures how early (leading) or late (dragging) it was, in milliseconds. Nothing is uploaded. Everything stays in this browser.
      
      
      Pick a sticking, press Start on the Play page and play along with the moving line. The numbers appear when you stop.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Quick start
      
      
        
        
      1. Settings: pick a tempo, use headphones for the click, tap Test microphone and hit the pad. The level bar should cross the trigger line on every stroke. Calibrating each stick is optional, but lets the app tell L from R.
        2. 
        
      2. Library: press New to write a routine or Import to bring one in. One exercise plays on its own. Two or more make a routine, played in order.
        3. 
        
      3. Play: set the tempo, click level and how long each exercise plays, then press Start. After the count-in, play on the moving line and press Stop when you are done. Your result appears first, with Again and Done. The staff keeps its colours and numbers so you can see which strokes were off. Done brings the controls back.
        4. 
      
      
      
      No microphone? Under Settings › Device, set Input to "Keyboard or pads" and press F for left, J for right. This is a test mode, and its timing is rougher than a pad with a microphone.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      The four pages and the editor
      
      
        
        
      • Play: tempo, click level, Start, Pause, Restart and Stop, the notation, the lead/drag gauge and your results.
        • 
        
      • Library: your saved routines, each with Edit and Play. New starts a routine, Import adds one from a file or a link, and Export saves the whole library as one file that Import reads back (it can also be hosted as a library). A title such as "Rudiment: Single stroke roll" groups the routine under Rudiment. Routines with no group go under Other. The ⋮ on a routine has Download and Delete. Delete asks for a second tap, and Undo follows. The chevron opens a routine to show its exercises.
        • 
        
      • An unsaved routine is what you are working on. It is kept as you type, but a saved routine changes only when you press Save. The app asks before you open another routine and lose unsaved work. The editor opens full screen from Edit, and Back returns you.
        • 
        
      • Progress: History lists your runs by exercise or routine, with Play. Scores shows each exercise (weakest first) and each routine: tempo, accuracy, trend, lead or drag, and evenness. Achievements shows your practice days for the last four weeks, a streak that forgives one rest day a week, and badges.
        • 
        
      • Settings: Preferences (on-time window, visual pulse, click volume, colours), Device (input, microphone level, stick calibration, latency) and Data (export, import, clear, storage, library links).

      Reading the Play page
      
      
        
        
      • Each row is one pass of an exercise. The top row is playing and the next passes sit beneath it. Rows slide up as each pass ends.
        • 
        
      • The moving line reaches each note exactly when it is due. Strike as it arrives.
        • 
        
      • Letter colours: green is on time, blue and slanted is early, pink and underlined is late, and a faded letter means no hit was heard. The on-time window in Settings sets how close counts as green.
        • 
        
      • An arrow tag such as →R means the stroke was due on the other hand but the app heard R. This needs both sticks calibrated.
        • 
        
      • The gauge shows your average lead or drag over the last 32 strokes. Left is leading, right is dragging.
        • 
        
      • The level bars appear when a run ends. Each bar is one stroke's loudness against your own typical stroke: the middle is typical, higher is louder, and blue bars are left hand. Scroll sideways for the whole run. The number is the spread in dB, so smaller is more even. With an accent or ghost rule only the normal strokes count, since accents and ghosts are checked separately. A line below says if one hand is quieter. Clipped hits are left out, and keyboard input has no levels.
        • 
        
      • Narrow screens: a busy pass wraps onto two lines at a barline.
        • 
        
      • Keys: Space starts, pauses and resumes, Enter plays Again after a run, and Escape stops.
        • 
        
      • Pause stops the click, and Resume counts you in from the start of that pass. Restart goes back to your start exercise.
        • 
        
      • Choosing where to start: with a routine, tap an exercise in the rows (or press ↑ ↓). After a run, scroll the staff to look back over every exercise.

      Click levels
      
        
      RLRR LRLL RLRR LRLL
      
        
      4/4: one bar, sixteen strokes
      EasyMediumHardExtreme
      Each mark is one stroke. A filled mark has a click.
      
        
      6/8: one bar, six eighths
      MediumHardExtreme
      Hard follows the two groups of three. Easy is every stroke, as above.
      
        
      Choose the click level on the Play page. Easy clicks every stroke (none on rests), Medium every beat, Hard the strong beats (1 and 3, or the start of each group), and Extreme beat 1 of each bar. Master only clicks the count-in, so you hold the tempo yourself. Each level is scored on its own, against the same grid. Set Scoring to Practice to try something without saving it: you still see the result, but nothing is added to your records. It is back on the next time you open the app. Use headphones, or the click is heard as strokes.
      
        
      
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Writing a routine
      
      
      L and R are strokes, a space starts a new beamed group and _ is a rest. One line is one exercise. Two or more lines make a routine, played in order. With no note code, strokes are sixteenths.
      
      
      A note code sets what the strokes after it are, in any time signature, until the next code: q quarter notes, 8 eighths, 16 sixteenths, 32 thirty-seconds, 8t eighth triplets, 16t sixteenth triplets, 5t quintuplets and 7t septuplets. 8 RLRL 16 RLRL is a beat of eighths, then a beat of sixteenths. The code has to give a sensible speed for the beat.
      
      
      Simple examples
      
      
      Each example has a Try it button.
      
      
      
        
      Four beats of sixteenths
      
        
      RLRL RLRL RLRL RLRL
      
        
      Four groups of four, one beat each. A barline falls every two beats.
      
        
      
      
      
      
        
      Two triplets in one beat
      
        
      8t RLR LRL
      
        
      Two groups of three, each with its own "3". The click accents each beat.
      
        
      
      
      
      
        
      Sixteenths then sextuplets
      
        
      16 RLRL 16t RLR LRL
      
        
      A beat of sixteenths, then a beat of sextuplets written as two triplets. 16t RLRLRL with no space gives one run of six, with a single "6".
      
        
      
      
      
      
        
      Rests
      
        
      16 R _ L _ R L _ L
      
        
      A rest takes time but expects no hit. A hit during a rest counts as an extra.
      
        
      
      
      
      
        
      Flams and drags
      
        
      8 fR fL dR dL
      
        
      f in front of a stroke is a flam and d a drag (two grace notes). The other hand plays the grace, so fR is a left grace into a right stroke. Play the main stroke on the click and the grace just before it, softer.
      
        
      
      
      
      Using the editor
      
      
      You do not have to type any of this. Builder and Text are two views of one routine. In Builder, pick a note code, then tap R, L, rest and space to add strokes. Tap flam or drag first to put grace notes on the next stroke. Each exercise has a ⋮ menu to insert, clone, move or delete it. In Text you edit the whole routine, one exercise per line, with line numbers beside any errors. The editor remembers your last view on each device.
      
      
      The ▶ beside Save plays the routine as it stands. The speaker button plays the exercise as sticks over the staff, with the grace strokes of flams and drags (Close or Esc to leave). In a routine, Title, Each exercise plays for, Time, Grouping and Default note apply to every exercise, and This exercise overrides them for the one you are editing.
      
      
      The routine format
      
      
      A routine is plain text, one exercise per line. A line starting with # is a note to yourself, and a name before a colon (Exercise 4:) is ignored. This app has no book content. You supply the exercises.
      
      
      title: My warm-up routine
each: 1m
RLRR LRLL
RLRL RLRL  x16
LRLL RLRR  90s  @100
Exercise 4: RRLL RRLL
16 RLRL 8t RLR LRL
      
      
      How long each exercise plays
      
      
        
        
      • each: sets the default for every exercise: 1m (a minute), 90s or x8 (eight passes). Without it, the "Each exercise plays for" setting applies.
        • 
        
      • strokes: sets the note code for every exercise after it, such as strokes: 8 or strokes: 8t. A code inside an exercise wins, and strokes: off goes back to sixteenths.
        • 
        
      • x16 at the end of a line plays that exercise 16 times. One pass is one play-through.
        • 
        
      • 90s or 1.5m plays for about that long, rounded to whole passes.
        • 
        
      • @100 plays that exercise at 100 BPM. Combine them: RLRL RLRL x16 @80.
        • 
      
      
      
      
        
      Passes: how x16 works
      
        
      title: Passes demo
each: x4
RLRL RLRL RLRL RLRL
LRLR LRLR LRLR LRLR  x8
RRLL RRLL RRLL RRLL  30s
      
        
      At 60 BPM a pass takes 4 seconds. The first exercise gets the default 4 passes and the second gets 8. The third plays for 30 seconds, which rounds to 8 passes.

LLRR LLRR LLRR LLRR

      Time signatures
      
      
      An exercise is as long as its strokes unless you add a time signature. Write it with a colon at the start of a line (7/8:), or on its own line as time: 7/8 for every exercise after it. time: off goes back to plain exercises, and an exercise's own signature wins.
      
      
        
        
      • The pulse is the beat you count: a quarter note in x/4, an eighth in x/8 and a half note in x/2, so 2/2 is cut time. BPM counts pulses. 7/8 at 120 BPM is 120 eighths a minute, and 2/2 at 60 BPM is 60 half notes a minute, as a cut-time book marks it. Only /2, /4 and /8 are supported.
        • 
        
      • Codes mean what they say. 8 is eighth notes in any signature. With no code, the strokes are shared evenly over the bar, so RLRLRLR in 7/8 is seven eighths and 14 strokes become sixteenths.
        • 
        
      • Grouping: 7/8 is felt as 2+2+3, 6/8 as 3+3 and 5/4 as 3+2. Write your own in brackets, such as 7/8 (3+2+2):. The groups must add up to the top number (7/8 (3+2) is refused). The click is strong on the first pulse and lighter at each group start, and the notation is beamed the same way. A line with its own spaces keeps your spacing. The Builder's Grouping menu offers the usual choices.
        • 
        
      • The count-in is one bar of the signature, with the same accents.
        • 
        
      • The exercise has to add up. It must fill one bar or a whole number of bars (two bars of 2/2 show a barline between them). If it does not, a note says so, such as "this exercise holds 5 eighth notes but 7/8 has 7". Use _ for rests. It still plays as written.
        • 
      
      
      
      
        
      Odd meters
      
        
      title: Odd meters
time: 7/8
RLRLRLR  x8
RLRLRLRLRLRLRL  x8
7/8 (3+2+2): RLRLRLR  x8
time: 6/8
RLRLRL  x8
RLRLRLRLRLRL  x8
time: 5/4
RLRLR  x8
      
        
      
      
      
      
        
      A cut-time book page
      
        
      title: Cut time
time: 2/2
strokes: 8
RLRL RLRL RLRL RLRL  x8
LRLR LRLR LRLR LRLR  x8
      
        
      Two bars of eighth notes, written as the page reads. The ¢ sign means a half-note beat, so BPM is the book's half-note tempo.
      
        
      
      
      
      Between exercises and the visual pulse
      
      
      Between exercises can be a 4-beat count-in or Straight on (the default). Straight on starts the next exercise with no pause, and only the notation marks the change: the rows slide up and the new exercise starts on the next beat.
      
      
      Visual pulse (Settings › Preferences) swells the moving line on every click, more on the first beat of a pass. Turn it off for a plain line.
      
      
      Colours (Settings › Preferences) switches to a colour-blind safe set: blue for leading, orange for dragging, grey for on time. Early notes also lean, late notes are underlined, and results at or above target carry a tick, so colour is never the only signal.
      
      
      Saving and importing
      
      
      Save keeps the routine in your library. If you change only the title, Save renames it. If you change the exercises, Save keeps the original and adds a new routine, so past runs still replay as played. The ⋮ beside Save has Save as new (a copy without changes) and Download file (a .txt to send).
      
      
      On the Library page, Import adds a routine from a .txt, .json or .csv file, or opens one from a link in the editor to review and Save. Text with errors opens in the editor to fix. New starts an empty exercise. In History, the bookmark on a routine saves it or deletes it from your library, and In library lists your library, including routines you have not played. A title such as "Rudiment: Single stroke roll" groups it under Rudiment on the Library page.
      
      
      JSON also works: a list such as ["RLRR LRLL", {"sticking":"RLRL RLRL","passes":16}], or an object with title, each and exercises.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Libraries and links
      
      
      On the Library page, press Import, choose Add a link… under Routine or library link, paste the address (and a name if you like) and press Load. A file with one routine opens in the editor to look over. Press Save to add it or Back to leave. A file with several routines is kept as a library in the dropdown, up to 20.
      
      
      Each routine in a library is tagged New or In library. Tap one to open it in the editor and Save it, or press Import all to add every routine not already in your library (routines with errors are skipped). A library you loaded recently shows without a request until you press Load. To rename a library, change its address or delete it, use Settings › Data › Library links. Export includes them.
      
      
      The Starter routines and Rudiments buttons on the Play page (shown until you have saved or played something) load two small libraries hosted next to the app: examples of the routine format, and the standard rudiments that can be written as sticking. The app has no other routines. You choose the library.
      
      
      The library file
      
      
      One plain text file. Write each routine as in the editor, with its own title: line, and put a line of three or more dashes (or an em dash) between routines. A routine without a title is named by its place in the file. Up to 100 routines are read.
      
      
      title: Singles
RLRL RLRL RLRL RLRL
---
title: Paradiddles
RLRR LRLL RLRR LRLL
      
      
      Hosting one
      
      
        
        
      • Put the file anywhere that serves plain text, such as a GitHub repository. Use its Raw button and paste that address (it starts with raw.githubusercontent.com).
        • 
        
      • The host must let other sites read the file. GitHub's raw addresses do. Many sites do not, and the app will say it could not reach the address.

      Scores, tempo and progress
      
      
        
        
      • On time is the share of due strokes inside your on-time window. With an accent or ghost rule a stroke must also be played as written: an accent clearly louder and a ghost clearly softer than its neighbours. An exercise needs at least one full pass to be scored.
        • 
        
      • Medals: on the Progress page each exercise gets a medal for each click level, from the fastest tempo you have cleared it at. It is measured in strokes a minute, so sixteenths count for more than eighths at the same BPM. Hickory is cleared at any speed, then Bronze at 120 strokes a minute, Silver 180, Gold 240, Platinum 320, Sapphire 400, Ruby 480 and Diamond 600. Sixteenths at 60 BPM is 240, so Gold.
        • 
        
      • Exercise table: after a routine run, each exercise gets its accuracy, average error and spread, plus your earlier best at that tempo.
        • 
        
      • The target is 90%. An exercise is "cleared" at a tempo when it reaches 90% there. After a run the app names the weak exercises and suggests staying at that tempo until they settle. Any tempo is always allowed.
        • 
        
      • Discard this run removes it from your records, for example after a bad microphone read.
        • 
        
      • Progress page: every exercise, weakest first, with accuracy at your last tempo, trend and lead/drag bias, grouped by click level. Play loads that exercise. A routine is cleared at a tempo when one run covered every exercise and all reached 90%.
        • 
        
      • Practise weak exercises: after a routine run, this plays only the exercises below target. Restart repeats the same ones. A partial run never counts as clearing the routine.
        • 
        
      • Tempo guidance: each exercise in the start list shows the tempo it has cleared, or "new". The summary shows where the whole routine has been cleared.
        • 
        
      • Staff of a run: each run keeps the result of every stroke. A run's page has a Staff group showing the notation with each stroke coloured and read out, and the Play page rebuilds its review from the same record.
        • 
        
      • History: on the Progress page, switch to History for your last 300 runs, by exercise or routine, newest first. Tap a group for its runs and a run for its exercises. Play loads the same routine, tempo, click level and settings, ready to start. Delete deletes the run. The ⋮ beside an exercise deletes just that exercise from the run, for one that was in the editor by mistake. An exercise nothing was heard in is listed as not played and is never scored. An untitled routine is called "Untitled 1", "Untitled 2" and so on. The pencil renames it everywhere, and a title typed in the editor replaces it. Runs beyond 300 are folded into your totals, streaks, achievements and cleared tempos, which are kept for good, but they can no longer be replayed.

      Settings details
      
      
        
        
      • Mic check: under Device, press it, stay quiet for two seconds, then hit the pad twelve times. Echo cancellation and noise suppression are switched off for this page so stick attacks stay sharp. It sets the trigger line and settle time, and says if the signal is too quiet, clipping, uneven or double triggering.
        • 
        
      • Trigger line: raise it if room noise counts as hits, lower it if soft strokes are missed.
        • 
        
      • Settle time: how long the app ignores the pad after a hit. Raise it if one stroke counts twice. Fast playing lowers it automatically so real strokes are not skipped.
        • 
        
      • Stick calibration: hit the same spot with one stick only, at normal strength, until the count finishes. Do both sticks.
        • 
        
      • Test left vs right: after calibrating, hit once with whichever hand is shown, 20 times in a shuffled order. It reports how often the app named the right stick, how each hand did, and how many hits a run would tag with the wrong hand. Below 90% is worth recalibrating.
        • 
        
      • Latency: sound reaches the page late, and the click reaches your ears late. Press Auto calibrate once on each device. It plays clicks through the speakers and times when the mic hears them, so you do not play. Calibrate by playing is the second choice: hit on each click, 80 hits or more, until the number settles. It assumes you were on the click on average. You can also type a number.
        • 
        
      • Accent and Other strokes (Play page) lay accents and ghost notes over the loaded exercise. Accent picks the loud strokes: the first of each group, each beat, each bar, every 2nd to 7th stroke (counted across passes, so it drifts if the count does not divide the pass), or the right or left hand. Rests are never accented. Other strokes can be Normal or Ghost. The staff shows accents with > and ghost notes in brackets.
        • 
        
      • How accents are scored: with the microphone, each accent is compared with the strokes around it and each ghost note with its neighbours. An accent must be about 4 dB louder and a ghost note about 6 dB quieter. Strokes less than 60 ms apart are not judged. Ghost notes are heard down to 14 dB below the trigger line (the dashed line on the Settings meter), kept at least 6 dB above the room noise from your last Mic check. A quiet hit counts only on a ghost note, so room noise elsewhere is ignored. The rule is saved with each run, and an exercise played with a different rule has its own scores and History group. Keyboard input does not judge loudness.
        • 
        
      • Flams and drags: only the main stroke is scored, as early or late against the click. Hits just before it (up to 80 ms for a flam, 120 ms for a drag) are its grace notes and are not counted as extras. While an exercise has them, the microphone also accepts a clearly louder strike soon after a hit, so a soft grace does not hide the main stroke. A grace as loud as the main stroke can, so play it softer. Keyboard play has no graces, so use one key per main stroke.
        • 
        
      • Records and storage: records are saved in this browser only. Export keeps a copy or moves it to another device, and Import brings it back. The app reminds you if you have never exported, or the last copy is two weeks old. On iPhone and iPad, Safari can clear a site's data after about a week without use, so add the page to your Home Screen. Settings › Data › Storage shows what takes space. Stroke detail (what rebuilds a run's staff) may use about 40% of what the browser allows. Past that, the oldest runs lose their detail first and keep their totals.
        • 
      
      
    
      

    
      
      
      If something is off
      
      
        
        
      • The microphone will not turn on: the page needs a secure address. Opening the file from your computer works in Chrome and Edge. Otherwise serve it with python3 -m http.server or switch Input to Keyboard.
        • 
        
      • Every stroke looks late or early: run Calibrate latency.
        • 
        
      • Extra hits show up: raise the trigger line, or use headphones for the click.
        • 
        
      • Wrong-hand tags look random: calibrate both sticks again on the same spot of the pad.
